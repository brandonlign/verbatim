How it was labeled

A native Chinese reader labeled 347 transcripts blind, with systems shuffled and unnamed. Re-labeling 71 lines matched the first round on 69.

How it works

Write it down. Cross-check it. Mark where to listen.

  1. 1

    Fine-tuned transcriber

    Whisper large-v3-turbo with its decoder trained on 8,387 real Mandarin–English clips, so it keeps the speaker's mix of languages.

  2. 2

    Cross-checks

    Regular Whisper decodes the same clip three ways: auto-detect, Mandarin forced, and English forced. Broken versions are dropped.

  3. 3

    Flag and underline

    A line is flagged when the model is unsure or the versions disagree. Inside each line, the least confident words are underlined.

Where it fails

What it still gets wrong.

Short phrases at a language switch

These can still get translated: 就是hard choice came out as this is hard choice.

When every decode makes the same mistake

Nothing disagrees, so nothing is flagged: 使用这个function的时候 became 使用这个方向的时候.

The flag catches about a quarter of errors

On the random sample, 11 of 42 lines with an error were flagged. Every flag was right, so a flag means listen again; no flag is not a guarantee.

Writing style carries over from ASCEND

Numbers come out as Chinese characters (ID七四零五六一七), which costs points on other datasets.

The flag doesn't transfer to other languages yet

It fires on 44% of English-only lines (60 of 75 had an error) and found no real errors in Spanish (0 of 11).
